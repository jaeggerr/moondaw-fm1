MOONDAW song editor

A song's instruments, and above all its sampled ones: the engine, the envelope, the filter, the layers, and the sample zones over the keyboard. Samples are global on the FM-1 — one bank for the whole machine — and an instrument is not: it lives inside the song file and nowhere else. So this page edits a .trk, writes it back exactly as the FM-1 would, and can send it to the device.

1 · The song

or drop one here. The FM-1's own songs come off the device with the installer page (“Save the songs on the FM-1”).

Served from the repository, the demo is one click away: SMPL-DEMO.trk — eight sampled instruments, from the drums' one zone to the piano's five.

2 · The sample bank

The bank is the machine's, not the song's: a zone names a sample by file name and the FM-1 plays whatever its bank has under that name, ignoring the folder, the .wav and the case. A name the bank has not got is silent, never an error — which is why this page wants to know the bank.

5 · The zones

The first zone holding a note wins, so the order of the table is the priority: what an earlier zone already answers is drawn red on the map and will never sound. Drag the LO and HI tabs; the keys at the foot play.

6 · The layers

Up to four, each a chip voice stacked on the instrument. A layer cannot be SMPL — the FM-1's loader refuses that one.

7 · Listen

The FM-1's own core, compiled to WebAssembly: the engines, the envelopes, the filter, the reverb and the delay are the device's, not a drawing of them. For a sampled instrument to sound, the packed bank file must be loaded above — the index read over the link names the samples but does not carry them.
