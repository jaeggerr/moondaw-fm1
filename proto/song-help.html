SONG, wearing the help

The help stops being a screen and becomes a coat: you switch it on, the view stays the view, and the foot of the panel says what the twelve buttons do right now — which changes as the cursor moves. It stays on until you switch it off.

The shape is settled and the words are not. A foot of two rows of six was chosen over a frame on four sides, for the reason below. What is still open is the vocabulary — six characters a cell — and whether SEL earns a cell at all.

Nothing here is firmware. The picture on the left below is the real SONG, read off the machine. Everything with a foot on it is drawn by hand, over SONG's own measurements: a 16 px head and rows of 28 (GRID_ROW).

The cost, first

Eight rows become six

SONG fills the panel to the pixel: a 16 px head and eight rows of 28 make exactly 240. There is no spare room, so the help has to take some. Two rows of 28 at the foot — one per row of the panel — leaves six phrases visible instead of eight.

The foot, and not a frame all round — chosen 02/11. A column down each side would cost a third of the width to hold two labels nobody can read sideways. The foot costs less and buys more, because the panel's buttons are two rows of six: draw two rows of six and the cell you read sits where the button sits under your thumb. There is nothing to look up — top right on the screen is top right under your hand.

What makes it worth the two rows

It changes under the cursor

A fixed list of shortcuts is what we already have, and it is why you were not convinced. This is not that: move the cursor off the pattern cell onto the phrase number and two dark cells wake up — SEQ copies the phrase below, LFO deletes it — while SEL stops saying PATTERN and starts saying STEPS. The screen tells you what is possible here, not what exists somewhere.

Dark is information too. A cell that never lights on this screen teaches the machine's shape faster than a page of prose: ARP and REC do nothing in SONG, and you learn it without being told.

EDIT held

The foot changes hands

While EDIT is down the twelve are not what you are about to press — the black keys are. So the foot swaps: the six OP keys and MONO, in the order they lie under the fingers, and the two things EDIT answers to on the panel. It goes when EDIT goes.

This is the piece I would build first even if you drop the rest. EDIT held is exactly the second you cannot remember whether cut is OP3 or OP4, and it needs no mode at all: you are already holding the button that asks for it.

  • It could be permanent. Nothing says this bar has to wait for the help to be on. Held EDIT is a deliberate gesture; showing the legend every time costs two rows for as long as the thumb is down, and no mode to remember.
  • MONO sits at the right because that is where it lies on the keyboard, well past OP6. The order on screen is the order under the hand.
Still open

Four things to shoot down

  • I kept SEL, against what you said. You called it obvious, and on most screens it is — but in SONG it is the one control that changes meaning four times: a pattern, a phrase's steps, what a REF plays, a section's name. Either it earns its cell or we admit the cell grid has a hole in it. Your call.
  • GLO is the project, not the mixer. FX opens the mixer. The example in your message had them swapped, and it is worth knowing it was the panel that misled you, not your memory: that is an argument for the coat.
  • Six characters a cell, and the drawing proved it. 40 px at CONDENSED 12. The first version of this page had SEL say PATTERN and ENV say SECTION, and the two words ran into each other on the screen. They are OPEN and SECT. here. SECT. is the failure mode, not the layout: an abbreviation nobody reads is worse than a dark cell, and if the vocabulary cannot be built at six characters the foot wants three rows of four instead — which costs a third row and breaks the match with the panel. That is the trade to settle on the machine.
  • OCT− and OCT+ are not in the grid. They are not on the two rows of six — they live by the knobs, left of the screen. Putting them in the grid would break the one rule that makes it work.
The real cost, said plainly

Every screen has to keep its word

The drawing is the easy half. The hard half is that each screen has to name its buttons, and go on naming them correctly as the cursor moves and the code changes. Each screen already declares which buttons are live — that is the mask behind the LEDs (ui_led_guide). What does not exist is the word, and the word that changes with the cursor.

That is where this could turn into the over-complicated thing you did not want. A line forgotten and the help lies, which is worse than no help. It is not a reason to drop it; it is the reason to start with one screen and EDIT's legend, and see whether keeping them true is cheap or a chore.
